### One thing a reviewer should look at first

**Whether the fail-closed posture for an unreadable terminal read is the right shape, and it is the amendment's sharpest judgement.** The owner proposed: not terminal, not a delivery, a recorded skip for that subject in that cycle, with the follow-up remaining outstanding under the existing bounded-retry posture. The shipped code says something subtly different about **blast radius**: a failed per-item read fails the whole binding's scan and retains its checkpoint, rather than dropping one candidate and advancing past it (`poller-events.ts:443-449`'s own reasoning — "dropping only the candidate would instead advance the window past an assignment nobody ever attributed"). The amendment takes the shipped shape, because checkpoint retention is what turns a skip into a one-cycle delay instead of a loss, and it says so. A reviewer who prefers per-subject isolation should note that the price is a checkpoint that advances past a follow-up nobody judged.

**The second is the reachability claim itself.** It is not argued, it is line-referenced: `poller-github.ts:226,276` send `state=open`, `tracking.ts:236` skips any row that is not `closed`, and the suite's proof (before `F-10` rewrote it) injected a closed row into a fixture the endpoint does not produce. The reviewer's question is whether that reasoning holds for **both** shapes — and it does not need to for the pulls feed, whose list carries no `merged` at all, which is recorded in `research.md` §R12.5 as a second, independent reason the read exists.

**The third is the audit reduction.** FR-035 named five rows and two are writable. That is a reduction in the obligation, not a loophole: `/v1/audit` is `method: 'GET'` and `appendAudit` has one caller surface, so the panel's outcomes have exactly one honest home and it is the document that already carries a dispatch attempt's own outcome. A reviewer should check that "the panel's record satisfies constitution IV" is stated as the answer rather than left as a silence where the obligation used to be.
