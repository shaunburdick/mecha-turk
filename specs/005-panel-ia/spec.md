## Problem Statement

The panel works and says so. Two issues became two worktree sessions; the loop from account to binding to dispatch to session to run list closed end to end on 2026-09-28. What it does not do is **tell the truth about itself**, and what it does with that truth is **spread across a spike's worth of surfaces that no longer describe the product**.

Three concrete failures, all visible to the operator in the first ten seconds:

1. **The status the panel shows is partly fiction.** `service/routes/status.ts` reports `paused: true`, `nextPollAt: null`, and `pausedReason: 'config-incomplete'` as **literals**, plus `repositories: []` and `agentPin.lastVerification: null` as fixed empties. The poll loop it describes is real, running, and re-reading its interval from configuration every cycle; a status document that says it is paused with no next poll and no bound repositories is a document contradicting the machine beneath it. 003 recorded this as out of scope and named it 005's work. An operator reading the panel right now cannot learn that polling is healthy — only that it is not.

2. **The navigation matches a prototype, not a product.** `src/panel-state.ts` carries `Repositories.activeTab: 'spike' | 'repos'`, and `src/panel-ui.ts`'s `repaintReposSection` implements the switch by writing `hidden` onto two containers. The "Spike" tab is the lifecycle experiment: an *Observed phase* select, a *Record phase* button, and the spike ledger list. The "Repositories" tab is the real product: bindings, accounts, the add form, and the runs list. Both are reachable, neither is the right shape, and the ledger and the dispatch history — two completely unrelated concerns — sit one tab apart with no shared header telling the operator what either one is.

3. **The dispatch list cannot answer the question the operator opens it to ask.** `src/runs-rows.ts`'s `canRetry` is `row.state !== 'dispatched'` — offered on `pending`, where a retry is meaningless because the run is already waiting. `GET /v1/events` caps at `MAX_LISTED_EVENTS = 100` with no cursor, no limit, and no filter, so the 101st dispatch is unreachable and nothing narrows the list. And 003's state model — `claimed`, `starting`, `unconfirmed`, `blocked:*`, `dead-lettered` — has no operator-readable label anywhere, because the list it would render in does not exist.

Underneath all three sits a fourth: **the product says "Runs" and "Repositories"; its owner has said "Dispatches" and "Bindings."** Every surface an operator reads should use the words the person who owns the product chose, and the rename was agreed on 2026-09-28 and deferred to this specification.

This specification replaces the spike-era surface with **six tabs — Status, Dispatches, Bindings, Accounts, Settings, About** — and makes each one an honest answer to one question. It **retires** the spike surface rather than hiding it, because a tab that only exists to be toggled off is a second way to reach the same capability and will drift from the first. It fixes the status projection. It pages and filters the dispatch list. It renders 003's states and 003's retry semantics, and 004's prompt field exactly once. It records, requirement by requirement, what it takes from its three predecessors and what it deliberately leaves alone.

## Governing Principles and Relationship to Features 002, 003, and 004

### How the amendment is sequenced

005 is a **consumer**, not a corrector. Unlike 003 — which superseded 002 because the shipped build contradicted 002's own text — nothing here finds the product violating a requirement it already states. What 005 does is take nine requirements its predecessors **deferred to it by name**, satisfy them, and give them a home:

| Deferred by | Requirement | Deferred because |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 003 §Out of Scope | status projection honesty (`paused` / `nextPollAt` / `repositories` / `agentPin`) | "belongs to 005's Status tab" |
| 003 §Out of Scope | runs-list cadence and the per-row retry affordance | "005 makes them discoverable and pleasant" |
| 003 §Out of Scope | the information-architecture rename | "lands in 005" |
| 003 FR-033 | the dead-letter operator affordance | "the per-row affordance landing in 005" |
| 003 FR-070 | "not listed?" project-picker guidance | placement belongs to the tab that holds the picker |
| 003 FR-071 – FR-073 | the first-run prerequisites section and its honest states | placement belongs to a tab; the behaviour is 003's |
| 003 FR-074 | every run state readable in the restructured surface | "the restructuring … lands in 005" |
| 003 FR-075 | the Dispatches/Bindings rename | "lands in 005" |
| 004 §Clarifications | the `Rule:` line reading as an assignment on a mention/review dispatch | "The copy fix belongs to 005 or later" |

So all three predecessors are **extended**, and the one **supersession** is 003's `## Wire Surface Delta` row for `Status`, which 003 marked "Unchanged" and 005 changes. Both directions use the same established shape: a version bump, a banner under the header, and a changelog.md section naming the requirement, the effect, and the authoritative text. **No predecessor's requirement text is edited.**

If the four documents are ever read as disagreeing about what the panel looks like, what it calls things, or what the status document reports, **this document prevails for the panel surface, the operator-facing vocabulary, and the status projection.** Their changelog.md sections are the index that proves it.

### Invariants this feature must not weaken

- **Read-only to GitHub stands (002 FR-031, 003 FR-002).** A tab is a place to look and to configure; nothing in it writes to GitHub. The natural-looking temptation — "resolve this from the Dispatches tab by acknowledging on GitHub" — is forbidden, exactly as it was in 003.
- **The impossibility requirement stands (003 FR-028).** 005 **retires the spike-era manual "Start session" control**. It started a session for the *current match* without a run, a lease, or a token, which 003 FR-035 forbids outright ("a panel MUST NOT infer dispatch eligibility from its own local state"). Its removal is therefore a conformance consequence of 003, not a taste decision (FR-018, FR-044).
- **Secret containment stands (002 FR-007, 003 NFR-106, 004 NFR-121).** The Accounts tab renders a credential-free DTO. A new operator string — the account display name (FR-066) — is validated and credential-shape-refused like every other operator string, and the secret-scan suites gain cases rather than exemptions.
- **`host.storage` namespaces stand (`AGENTS.md` invariant 4).** No key is renamed. `mecha-turk:project`, `mecha-turk:evidence`, and `mecha-turk:ledger` keep their names; the panel id stays `mecha-turk`; renaming either is a user-visible storage-namespace reset and is treated as a breaking change (FR-025).
- **The extension-spike-1 wire contract stands (`AGENTS.md` invariant 10).** The evidence schema version and the NDJSON event contract are compatibility surfaces. 005 changes how they are *read*, never their shape or version.
- **Committed bundles ship (`AGENTS.md` invariant 1).** Any change under `src/`, `panel/*.ts`, or `service/*.ts` ends with `npm run build` and the rebuilt bundles committed in the same commit (FR-087).
- **Capabilities stay `sessions` and `prompt` (`AGENTS.md` invariant 3).** `contributes.service` gains no `permissions` key (FR-079).

## Architecture Impact

005 changes **where the panel puts things**, not what the service is. Two modules change shape, and both for the same reason — a hardcoded value that a running process contradicts:

| Module | Today | After 005 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `service/routes/status.ts` | `polling: { intervalMs, nextPollAt: null, paused: true, pausedReason: 'config-incomplete' }`; `repositories: []`; `agentPin.lastVerification: null` | `polling` computed from the live timer; `repositories` one row per stored binding with its scan state; `agentPin.lastVerification` widened to the most recent verification the service holds, or an explicit *not available* marker |
| `service/routes/events.ts` | `MAX_LISTED_EVENTS = 100`, newest-first, no query parameters | cursor pagination and server-side filtering over the same newest-first order; `recentRuns()` becomes the unpaged primitive the paged read builds on |
| `service/routes/health.ts` | `SERVICE_VERSION = '0.0.1'`, pinned to `package.json` by test | **unchanged** — it becomes the About tab's single version source (FR-074) |
| `src/panel-ui.ts` | `repaintReposSection()` flips `section.spike.hidden` / `section.repos.pane.hidden` | both write paths deleted; one `activeTab` on the runtime drives one tab strip |
| `src/panel-state.ts` | `Repositories { activeTab, bindings, accounts, …, runs }` | `activeTab` lifted to the runtime as the shell's single navigation state; `repos` renamed `bindings`; the spike lifecycle fields (`pendingPhase`) leave the state |
| `src/repos*.ts`, `src/runs*.ts` | module names | `src/bindings*.ts`, `src/dispatches*.ts` (FR-024) |
| `src/runs-rows.ts` | `canRetry = row.state !== 'dispatched'` | replaced by a state→affordance table covering 003's model (FR-044) |

No new store, no new file, no new host call, no new manifest capability.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 — Know, in one glance, whether the machine is working (Priority: P1)

The panel opens on **Status**. It says the service is healthy, how long it has been up, where its data lives, that storage is writable, that three accounts are connected and one is rate-limited, that four bindings are scanning and one is not scanning because its credential is unusable, that the poll interval is 60 seconds and the next poll is in 41, and that the Default Agent pin is **not checkable by the panel** until a dispatch has been verified. Nothing on it is a placeholder.

**Why this priority**: it is the first thing the operator sees and the first thing they check when something is wrong. Today it reports a state the running process contradicts, which is worse than reporting nothing: it teaches the operator to distrust the panel.

**Independent Test**: mount the panel against a fake host and a temp-dir store with one healthy account, one rate-limited account, one binding scanning and one skipped; assert the Status tab renders each of those from the status document and that no literal `paused: true` / `nextPollAt: null` reaches the response when the poll loop is running. Then advance a **driven clock** past the reported `intervalMs` with the tab active, and assert the projection is re-read and the stamp moved; switch to another tab, advance three further periods, and assert no read was issued (FR-100).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a running service with three accounts and four active bindings, **When** the operator opens the panel, **Then** the Status tab shows service health, uptime, data directory, storage writability, one row per account with its connection state, one row per binding with its last scan and pending count, the effective interval with a real next-poll stamp, and the agent pin as *not checkable*.
2. **Given** the poll loop is running, **When** the status document is read, **Then** `paused` is `false`, `nextPollAt` is a future RFC 3339 stamp, and `pausedReason` is empty — none of the three is a literal.
3. **Given** an account whose credential GitHub rejected, **When** the operator reads Status, **Then** the account row shows `auth-failed` with the exact cause, and every binding bound to that account shows the consequence on its own row.
4. **Given** `service.storage.writable === false`, **When** the operator reads Status, **Then** a blocking notice explains that handoff cannot proceed, and the Accounts tab's token input stays disabled with the reason visible.
5. **Given** the Default Agent pin has never been verified, **When** the operator reads the prerequisites list, **Then** it reads *not checkable by the panel* and says the first dispatch is what checks it — never "ok".
6. **Given** the operator leaves the panel on Status and does nothing, **When** the poll interval elapses, **Then** the values on the tab are those of a fresh read, the tab says it re-reads on that period, and the last-read stamp moves — with no button press anywhere (FR-100, v1.16.0).
7. **Given** the service stops answering while Status is open, **When** the next period elapses, **Then** the last good values stay on screen marked stale with the cause named and `Refresh status` still offered, and the period does not change (FR-019, FR-100).

### User Story 2 — Work out what happened to a dispatch, and do something about it (Priority: P1)

The operator opens **Dispatches** and sees a paged, newest-first list. Each row names the repository, the subject, the trigger, the state in words with a reason underneath, the attempt, the target project and worktree, and the session if one exists. A run that failed says *Retry*; a run blocked on a missing project says *Retry* once the project is selected and is otherwise disabled with the cause; a run that is unconfirmed says **Resolve** and offers the two explicit answers with the project, worktree, and attachment id to check; a run that is dead-lettered offers *Return to waiting* and says the attempt count resets. The list pages past 100 rows and filters to one binding or one state.

**Why this priority**: 003 made the states correct and the transitions reachable; nothing an operator looks at is discoverable without this tab. A retry that appears on a run already waiting teaches the operator that the panel does not know its own queue.

**Independent Test**: render the tab against run-history fixtures covering every state in 003's `## Dispatch State Model`; assert each renders a label plus a reason, that Retry appears on exactly `failed` and cleared `blocked:*`, that `unconfirmed` shows Resolve and not Retry, and that a 100-row fixture pages.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a run in `failed` with reason "the dispatch created no session", **When** the operator selects it, **Then** *Retry* is offered, and activating it returns the run to waiting under the same run key with the attempt incremented and an audit row written.
2. **Given** a run in `pending`, **When** the operator looks at it, **Then** *Retry* is **absent**, not disabled, and the reason reads *already waiting for a panel*.
3. **Given** a run in `unconfirmed`, **When** the operator looks at it, **Then** *Retry* is absent and **Resolve** is offered; choosing *this dispatch created no session* is the only path that re-dispatches, and the panel names the project, worktree option, and attachment id to check first.
4. **Given** a run in `blocked:project-missing` whose project is still unregistered, **When** the operator looks at it, **Then** *Retry* is disabled and the reason line names the unresolved project, and no requeue budget is consumed.
5. **Given** a run in `dead-lettered`, **When** the operator activates *Return to waiting*, **Then** the panel states that the attempt count resets before it happens, and the run returns to waiting with the attempt at 1.
6. **Given** more than one page of dispatches, **When** the operator opens the tab, **Then** the page shows which range it is showing, offers Next and Previous, and preserves the operator's position across a refresh.
7. **Given** a filter that yields nothing, **When** the operator reads it, **Then** it says the filter matched nothing, not that there are no dispatches.
8. **Given** a run with three source references, **When** the operator opens the row, **Then** every reference is listed with its kind, origin, link, and detection time, and the one that arrived after the dispatch was authorized is marked as not necessarily seen by the agent.

### User Story 3 — Bind a repository and see exactly what it will do (Priority: P1)

The operator opens **Bindings**, adds `owner/name`, picks an account from the Accounts tab's list, picks a target project from the host's own list — or finds the project they want is not listed and reads the three manual ways to register it without leaving the panel — enables the triggers they want, chooses a worktree option, and writes the starting prompt that every dispatch for this repository will read first. That prompt appears **once**, in the binding's editor, and nowhere else in the panel. The row shows when this binding last scanned, what it is waiting on, and whether it is enabled.

**Why this priority**: this is the operator's primary configuration act and the reason the product exists. 004 landed the field specifically so this tab would render it; getting it wrong here — twice, or on the summary line — undoes that.

**Independent Test**: drive the tab against the fake host and a temp-dir store; assert a full create/enable/disable/edit cycle round-trips through `GET`/`PUT /v1/bindings`, that exactly one element in the rendered tab carries the prompt, and that a credential-shaped prompt is refused with its remediation.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a fresh store, **When** the operator creates a binding with a repository, account, project, triggers, and worktree option, **Then** it appears in the list with a last-scan line reading *not scanned yet* and a pending count of zero.
2. **Given** a project the operator wants that `host.listProjects()` does not return, **When** the operator activates *not listed?*, **Then** the panel states command palette → Add project, sidebar **+**, and folder browser, and the binding stays in its recoverable state until a registered project is selected.
3. **Given** a binding with a starting prompt of 340 characters, **When** the operator opens its editor, **Then** exactly one field carries the prompt; the row summary shows its presence and length and never its text or its fingerprint.
4. **Given** a prompt containing a credential-shaped string, **When** the operator saves, **Then** the service refuses it with a field-level remediation and the previously stored prompt stays in force.
5. **Given** a binding whose account was removed, **When** the operator reads the list, **Then** the row renders disabled with the reason *account removed*, not as an inert row.
6. **Given** an invalid submission, **When** the operator saves, **Then** every other binding is byte-identical afterwards and the refusal names the field and the remediation without echoing what was submitted.

### User Story 4 — Know which accounts can poll, and fix the ones that cannot (Priority: P1)

The operator opens **Accounts**. Each account shows a display name, the GitHub login, the numeric id, its lifecycle state, its connection state, when it was last verified, and the four-capability scope matrix with each capability reading ok, missing, or unknown. One account is `auth-failed`; the operator rotates its token and the checkpoints, deliveries, dispatches, and audit history for that account are all still there. The operator adds a second account through the same paste-and-connect flow they used the first time — under the always-visible disclaimer, with no step to accept and nothing to re-ask. *(Was: "…through the same consent-and-handoff flow they used the first time — consent already given is not asked again." — re-cut at v1.7.0 with the dialog.)* Removing an account is two clicks, and the first click says that two bindings will be disabled.

**Why this priority**: an account that cannot poll is the most common way this product stops working, and today the panel's only account surface is the spike's handoff group. Custody must move to a real tab without moving a single credential.

**Independent Test**: render against account DTO fixtures covering all six lifecycle states and all four connection states; assert the DTO carries no credential member, that rotation retains history, and that the delete confirmation names the cascade.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an account in `auth-failed`, **When** the operator reads the row, **Then** it shows the exact cause and names *rotate the token* as the remediation, and every binding bound to it shows that it cannot poll.
2. **Given** an account in `pending_handoff`, **When** the operator reads the row, **Then** it is distinguishable from `error` with reason `interrupted-handoff` and both offer the same remediation: complete or replace the handoff.
3. **Given** an operator who has connected an account before, **When** they add a second account, **Then** the flow is identical — paste token, connect — with the same static disclaimer visible and no gate to pass again. *(Was: "Given an operator who already gave consent … they are not asked for consent again." — re-cut at v1.7.0.)*
4. **Given** two bindings bound to one account, **When** the operator clicks *Remove account* once, **Then** the button arms and states that two bindings will be disabled; only a second click deletes, and the bindings remain in the list, disabled with that reason.
5. **Given** an account with an operator display name, **When** the GitHub login is renamed upstream, **Then** the login updates and the display name is untouched.
6. **Given** any account DTO, **When** the panel renders it, **Then** no credential member exists on the object, and the secret-scan suites pass unchanged.

### User Story 5 — Get to any of the six things in one click (Priority: P2)

The panel has one horizontal tab strip and six tabs: **Status, Dispatches, Bindings, Accounts, Settings, About**. Each answers exactly one question, each says when it was last read, and none of them is reachable two ways. The spike-era "Spike" tab and its observed-phase recorder and ledger list are gone from the tab strip, not tucked behind it.

### Edge Cases

- **The status read fails entirely.** The Status tab must say the service did not answer, name it as the source of what is unknown, and keep its static content. It must not show zeros, empty lists, or a reassuring summary built from a failed read.
- **The service is `degraded` because the store is unavailable.** `storage.writable` is `false`, `schemaVersion` is `null`, and no account or binding list can be read. Status says exactly that and does not render an empty account list as if the operator had none.
- **`nextPollAt` is in the past.** The timer has not fired yet — a long scan, a suspended machine, a throttled tab. The panel shows the stamp as overdue rather than silently substituting the configured interval, and a stall longer than the interval is visible rather than inferred.
- **Polling is paused for a reason outside the closed vocabulary.** The panel renders the machine-readable reason verbatim rather than mapping an unknown code to a friendly guess.
- **A binding is disabled and its account is active.** Both lines must be correct and distinguishable; "disabled" alone is not an explanation.
- **Two bindings on the same repository under different accounts.** The row keys on the binding, and both may appear; nothing merges them.
- **The operator removes an account that is bound to nothing.** The cascade line says zero bindings will be disabled, and the two-step still applies because removal is irreversible.
- **The operator removes the last account.** Every binding becomes unable to poll, Status reports `no-active-bindings` as the paused reason, and the prerequisites list names the missing account.
- **The bindings list is empty and the accounts read has not succeeded** — at mount, in flight, or after a failed read that followed a successful read of an empty list. This is the frame where **all three** defects this block exists to end are available at once, and the one place a gate and a string can satisfy different requirements and still leave the operator looking at a lie. So three things must hold **at once**. The gate does **not** fire, because the absence of an answer is not the absence of accounts (FR-120) and an unread list is **not evidence of an absence**. The empty text does **not** name *New binding*, because the read-state condition has that control disabled (FR-122's third row) — naming an unavailable control and leaving no reason beside it is the reassuring-lie shape FR-083 exists to prevent, in a narrow window and therefore easy to miss in review. **The row does name Refresh, and Refresh is itself unavailable during `loading`** — the accepted, bounded overlap: one render of a state that resolves on its own, actionable again at `idle` and after a failed read, and **not** to be removed by ungating Refresh, which would be a read-behaviour change belonging to its own requirement (FR-122, clarification row 61, `005 AC-157`). And the empty text does **not** assert that the operator has **no** accounts, because the panel has not established that; it says the account list is **not known** and names **Refresh** — FR-120's accounts gate never disables it, though the in-flight window does at `loading` (`005 AC-157`). The **stale-list** variant is the sharp one: a failed read after a successful read of an empty list leaves the list empty on the panel's own state, so a selector keyed on length rather than on read-success renders the *add an account* row — asserting an absence over an unanswered read, which is the same defect one layer down (FR-122's consistency rule, `005 AC-156`). The tab's own failed-read channel keeps carrying its cause and a retry where there is one (FR-019), and none of these strings restates it (FR-121's channel rule).
- **The account list is unreadable, and the bindings list is not empty.** The two must not be confused. An unreadable account list renders its cause and a retry and stays visibly distinct from a successful read (FR-019), the gate does not fire (FR-120), the reason line is absent, and none of FR-122's empty-text strings appears — because the list is not empty, so there is no empty message to govern, and the rows already render with their own states.
- **The account list empties while the add form is already open.** The gate closes behind an editor the operator is still looking at. The form does not silently vanish and the submission is not left with a reason the panel no longer believes: the refusal carries FR-123's first row, which is the same sentence as FR-121's line because it is the same reason.
- **Accounts exist but every one of them is unusable.** The picker is empty and *correctly* so, *New binding* stays enabled because the gate is about accounts at all, the picker field's placeholder reads *Select an active account* — the state it actually lists, and the only one of `AccountState`'s six it can offer — the refusal names the fix or replace remediation rather than a selection the operator cannot make, and the field stays disabled (FR-123). An empty picker here is not the defect. A picker that offers nothing, asks for a **verified** account the product has no state for, and then tells the operator to **pick** from it, is.- **A binding is saved while a dispatch for it is in flight.** The dispatch's snapshot — project, worktree option, starting prompt — is unaffected (004 FR-015), and the Dispatches row must not visibly change as a result of the edit.
- **A starting prompt is cleared.** Only an explicit empty value, an explicit `null`, or an empty-after-trim clears it; omitting the field preserves it (004's confirmed omission-preserves rule). The panel's save MUST therefore send the field explicitly whenever the operator cleared it, and MUST NOT send it as absent by accident.
- **The credential-shape refusal fires on the display name.** It must name the field, not the value, and the previously stored display name must stay in force.
- **A run's state is a value this panel version does not know.** It renders *unknown state* with the raw value shown, never a blank row and never a guess. Forward compatibility beats a tidy label on a state that arrived from a newer service.
- **A stored row predates 003's migration table.** It projects through the non-destructive mapping into 003's vocabulary and the panel renders the projected state; the panel never renders the raw `pending | in-flight | dispatched` token.
- **The dispatch list is empty because the filter excluded everything.** The empty state names the filter and offers to clear it.
- **The service cannot supply an honest total.** The panel shows the range it has and says the total is unavailable, rather than showing the page size as if it were the total.
- **A page boundary is crossed by new dispatches arriving.** Pagination is over a snapshot the service labels; a row that moved is not silently dropped, and a refresh returns the operator to the page they were on rather than resetting to the first.
- **The operator activates Retry twice quickly.** One action runs; the second is refused by the existing busy gate; the row is not optimistically flipped before the service answers.
- **The retry is refused by the service.** The refusal renders with its distinct reason and the row stays exactly as the last read reported it.
- **The correlation id cannot be copied.** The copy affordance is unavailable and says why, rather than copying nothing.
- **The audit read returns rows whose entity the panel does not recognise.** Rows render as read-only text, never dropped and never reinterpreted.
- **Settings renders with a config the panel cannot fully parse.** Every field that did parse renders; every field that did not renders as *unreadable* with the remediation, and the panel does not fall back to showing a default as though it were configured.
- **About renders before the first status read.** It shows the static identity content immediately and the service-derived fields as *not yet read*, never as empty strings that read like a blank version.
- **The operator is on a narrow frame.** No primary action requires horizontal scrolling; tab labels truncate rather than wrap into a second row that changes the strip's height.
- **The panel is torn down mid-dispatch.** Disposal is idempotent, the relay loop's in-flight request is abandoned rather than retried by a second loop, and no handle is disposed twice.
- **The Status tab's first read fails, so no interval is ever learned (v1.16.0).** No tick is armed and the tab says it is not refreshing itself. The operator can still read on demand with `Refresh status`, and a later **successful** read that carries an interval arms the tick without a remount — the panel does not need the tab to be re-activated, and it does not need the panel to be reopened (FR-100, FR-101).
- **The panel is suspended, or the tab is throttled, longer than the interval (v1.16.0).** A tick that fires late reads once and the next tick is scheduled from the timer, not from a backlog: there is no catch-up burst, because a burst is the failure mode a cadence is supposed not to have (FR-100).
- **A tick fires while the operator is mid-interaction on another tab (v1.16.0).** It cannot: the tick is disarmed the moment another tab becomes active, so the case is the ordinary one. Recorded because the *inverse* is the defect to guard — a tick that outlived its tab would repaint a background tab under the operator's hands (FR-100, FR-017).
- **The status document reports an interval outside the documented bounds (v1.16.0).** The panel arms the period the document reports, because the service owns the value and its validator is the only authority on what is acceptable (002 FR-024, FR-052's rule reused). The panel neither clamps the value to the Settings tab's displayed bounds nor re-validates it; a value the service could not have written does not occur, and clamping would put a period on the tick that no document ever said.
- **`host.storage` was wiped.** Selection state is absent, the panel falls back to its documented default, and nothing durable is reported as missing because of it — durable state lives in the service store (002 FR-034).

#### A. Authority, scope, and invariants

This specification **supersedes** exactly one row of 003's `## Wire Surface Delta` — the `Status` operation — and extends everything else it touches. Every other requirement of 002, 003, and 004 stands unchanged and is **referenced, not restated**.
- **FR-002**: The extension and the service MUST remain **read-only with respect to GitHub** (002 FR-031, 003 FR-002). No requirement in this specification may be satisfied by a GitHub write. A tab MUST NOT offer an acknowledgement, comment, label, review, or any other GitHub state change as a way to resolve, deduplicate, or reconcile a dispatch.
- **FR-003**: The system MUST fail closed in the panel the way it fails closed in the service (002 FR-024, 003 FR-003, constitution II). Every unmet prerequisite, every unresolved project, every unusable credential, and every unreachable service MUST be a **visible state with a named cause and a remediation**. The panel MUST NOT render a missing value as a healthy default, and MUST NOT render an unknown value as a reassuring one.
- **FR-004**: This specification MUST NOT introduce a host capability, API, permission, or private interface (003 NFR-110, `AGENTS.md` invariant 3). `capabilities[]` remains exactly `sessions` and `prompt`; `contributes.service` gains no `permissions` key; the manifest id remains `mecha-turk`; the SDK pin and engine floor are unchanged.
- **FR-005**: The upgrade MUST NOT quarantine, discard, or reset any binding, account, checkpoint, delivery, run, audit row, or `host.storage` entry, and MUST NOT change the meaning of any stored value. A value written by an earlier version MUST continue to read as what it was. This includes the `pending | in-flight | dispatched` queue vocabulary, which continues to project through 003's non-destructive migration table rather than being rewritten in place.
- **FR-006**: **The Accounts add form is the only supply surface for an account's optional expected-login constraint** (002 FR-009). The form MUST offer exactly one optional, non-credential input — *expected GitHub login* — submitted as the existing `expectedLogin` member of the credential-verify request, which the service already validates and stores against the account; **absent or empty means no constraint**, and the account is then created exactly as it is today. The manifest integration card MUST NOT carry this input: 002 FR-041 removes the card's `expected-login` setting as a single-repo-era duplicate, because one card field cannot hold a per-account constraint across N accounts. Nothing else about the flow changes — the storage pre-flight, the one-shot handoff, and the two-step refusal are untouched (FR-060, FR-061); the consent gate and consent text this clause used to name left with the dialog at v1.7.0 (002 v1.9.0), the constraint's semantics are 002's and unchanged (a mismatch with `/user` rejects the account fail-closed; identity stays keyed on the numeric user id, and the constraint never becomes a durable key or a display label — 002 FR-009, FR-066), and the input renders as an ordinary optional field with the service, never the panel, as the validation gate (FR-052's rule, 002 FR-024).

#### B. The six-tab shell

- **FR-010**: The panel MUST present exactly six top-level tabs, in this order: **Status, Dispatches, Bindings, Accounts, Settings, About**. Each answers exactly one operator question and MUST NOT present a second tab that answers the same question.
- **FR-011**: The shell MUST be built on the host SDK's tab primitive, so that selection semantics, keyboard behaviour, and accessible association come from the SDK rather than from hand-written visibility toggling. The current mechanism — `repositories.activeTab: 'spike' | 'repos'` driving `section.spike.hidden` and `section.repos.pane.hidden` in `repaintReposSection` — MUST be **deleted**, not extended. After this feature there MUST be no code path that hides a spike-era container to switch surfaces, and the spike-era surface MUST NOT exist as a tab, a pane, or a fallback (FR-011 is what "retire, not hide" means: a hidden second path to a capability is a second thing that can drift).
- **FR-012**: Exactly one tab body MUST be active at a time. Activation state is a single field on the panel runtime; there MUST NOT be a second, independently-derived notion of "what is showing."
- **FR-013**: A tab body MUST be mounted on its **first activation** and MUST remain mounted thereafter. It MUST NOT be mounted before its first activation: the host clears subscriptions on unmount, pause, remove, and server switch, so mounting all six on panel open would open six independent read paths and make every one of them something to tear down correctly. Every mounted body MUST be disposed on teardown (FR-017).
- **FR-014** *(re-cut at v1.16.0 — GitHub issue #20 made Status a scoped exception to the no-read-on-re-activation rule; superseded wording recorded in changelog.md → `### v1.16.0`)*: **Activation is idempotent for five tabs and reads immediately for the sixth.** Activating the already-active tab MUST be a no-op that performs no service read on **Dispatches, Bindings, Accounts, Settings, and About**; on those five, refreshing a tab is an explicit action the operator takes, not a side effect of looking at it. **Status is the one exception: every activation of the Status tab MUST issue a read, including a re-activation of the already-active tab**, because a status an operator is watching must not be a status frozen at the moment they looked at it (FR-100). Every activation MUST remain side-effect-free otherwise: an activation MUST NOT mutate any service-held state, MUST NOT create, delete, or change a project, worktree, session, agent, binding, or account (FR-089), and MUST NOT change the active tab, the mounted-tab set, or which tab bodies are disposed. **Every tab MUST show when it was last read**, and that stamp asserts exactly one thing: **how old the data currently on screen is**. With a repeating read in play the stamp can no longer distinguish a manual refresh from a tick, so it MUST NOT be presented as if it could — no *you refreshed this*, no *updated automatically*, no per-tick incrementing counter, and no separate automatic-refresh stamp beside it. A failed read does not advance the stamp: the stamp stays at the last **successful** read, so the age it reports is the age of the content actually displayed (FR-019). **The Status tab's `Refresh status` button remains a way to force an immediate read** and is the operator's own cadence-independent path to fresh data at any time (FR-100).
- **FR-015**: The panel MUST open on **Status** and MUST NOT restore the previously active tab across mounts. The reason is specific: the operator opens this panel because something happened, and a panel that reopens on Bindings hides the answer to the question that made them open it. `host.storage` already hosts `last-viewed` UI state (002 FR-034) and this requirement simply declines to use it for the active tab; no key is added and none is removed.
- **FR-016**: The tab strip MUST be operable by keyboard alone with visible focus. Each tab MUST expose an accessible name equal to its visible label, the active tab MUST be programmatically marked as selected, and each tab body MUST be programmatically associated with its tab so that a screen reader announces "Dispatches, tab, 2 of 6" and the body's context together.
- **FR-017**: Teardown MUST dispose every handle mounted by all six tabs, remove every node they created, clear every timer they started, and release every host subscription they registered — idempotently, and in an order that does not depend on which tab was active. A torn-down panel MUST leave no orphan node in `#root`, no surviving interval, and no retained runtime reference.
- **FR-018**: **Exactly one relay loop exists, and tab switching does not change whether it runs.** The claim → dispatch → report loop MUST be owned by exactly one owner — the Dispatches tab, or the panel root on its behalf, stated explicitly in the plan — and MUST NOT be created, destroyed, or duplicated by a tab switch. Switching away from Dispatches while a dispatch is in flight MUST NOT abandon, duplicate, or re-claim that dispatch. This is the single highest-risk regression in a shell that mounts and unmounts the surface owning the loop.
- **FR-019**: Every tab MUST display its own read state — loading, loaded with a stamp, or failed with the cause and a retry affordance — and MUST NOT render an empty result as though it were a successful empty result. A failed read MUST retain the last successfully read content visibly marked as stale, or state plainly that there is none. **A read the repeating cadence started MUST fail exactly as a manually started read fails, with no distinction visible to the operator.** It MUST keep the last successfully read document in place, visibly marked stale; MUST name the exact cause; MUST offer the same retry affordance the manual path offers — on Status, the `Refresh status` button, which is not disabled by a failing cadence and remains the operator's own way to force an immediate read (FR-014); and MUST leave the last-read stamp where the last **successful** read put it, so the age it reports keeps describing the content on screen (FR-014). A failing cadence MUST NOT: blank or empty the tab; increment, stack, or append an error notice per tick; replace the last document with an error state; change the cadence's period, back off, or lengthen itself in response to failures; retry the failed read sooner than the next tick; or report success it did not get. A tab whose reads are failing keeps showing the last good document and the reason it is old — which is the honest description — rather than degrading into a blank surface or a stream of repeating errors while the operator reads (constitution IV, NFR-111, NFR-112).

"Run" and "Repositories" MUST NOT appear in any string a human reads. - **FR-022**: **The internal domain terms `run`, `run key`, `run ordinal`, and `attempt` are retained.** 002 FR-030's deterministic run key, 003 FR-010's run-key tuple and ordinal, 003 FR-020's dispatch-attempt identity and token derivation, 004 FR-038's correlation derivation, and the `run.` audit entity are all defined over "run" and are **unchanged**. The product owner's decision to rename "service route names" is therefore honoured for the routes that are **already correctly named** (`/v1/bindings`) and **deferred** for the ones that are not, recorded in `## Out of Scope` and raised as Gate Question 1.

#### I. Rendering, accessibility, and lifecycle

- **FR-080**: Every service-supplied and source-supplied string MUST render through the SDK's non-HTML path (`textContent` and the SDK UI primitives). #### J. The actor allow-list (added at v1.11.0; GitHub issue #9; **FR-092 and FR-093 re-cut and FR-096 added at v1.14.0**)

The field and its validation rules are **002 v1.11.0's** (FR-047); the gate that enforces them is **003 v1.8.0's**. This block is **rendering only**. Its centre of gravity is a single obligation the product owner attached to the fail-open posture: an absent allow-list means *any human may trigger*, and that state MUST be **discoverable — visible in the panel and warned about — rather than silently implying protection**. Every requirement here exists to make one sentence of copy impossible to say wrongly.

**v1.14.0 completes that sentence's failure modes.** A sentence of copy can be wrong in three ways, and v1.11.0 ruled out only the first: it can imply a **control** that does not exist (FR-092, NFR-113); it can assert a **capability** the binding does not currently have — telling a *disabled* binding it can start sessions (FR-092 as re-cut, NFR-114); and it can describe a **surface** the operator does not have, by naming acts the binding's trigger switches do not watch (FR-096). The first is the reassuring lie. The second and third are the alarming one, and they are the worse of the two for the operator, because a panel that overstates its own exposure trains people to stop reading it.

- **FR-090**: The binding editor MUST present the allow-list as **one field**, added to FR-053's field list, labelled in the product's own vocabulary as the set of GitHub logins allowed to trigger dispatches from this repository. Its guidance MUST state, in the field's own words and without the operator opening anything else, the three states 002 FR-047 defines: **no list configured → anyone who can open an issue or comment on this repository may start a session** *(the rendered clause for this first state is **superseded at v1.14.0 by FR-096's derivation** — the obligation to state the state is unchanged, but the fixed sentence above is withdrawn as rendered copy because it names acts only the `mention` trigger watches; `ALLOWED_USERS_GUIDANCE` is the string this affects)*; **a list → exactly those logins**; and **an empty list is not "nobody"** — it is refused, because the way to stop every trigger is to disable the binding. The field is **free text the operator supplies**: there is no GitHub identity picker, because the host offers no such API and this document may not invent one (FR-004, FR-089), and the service offers none either. The service is the **only** validator (002 FR-024), and the panel MUST NOT pre-emptively accept input the service would refuse nor pre-emptively reject input it would accept (FR-052's rule, reused verbatim).
- **FR-091**: **One rendering per list value — count only, never the login text.** The allow-list's logins MUST be rendered **exactly once in the entire panel**: as the editor field's own value. They MUST NOT appear on a row summary, on Status, on a dispatch row, in a Diagnostics view, or in a second editor. A **row summary MAY show the count** of allowed logins, and MUST NOT show the login text — the same presence-and-length-only rule the starting prompt follows (FR-051, 004 FR-052, `src/bindings-prompt.ts`'s row summary). FR-051's rule is **applied, not relaxed**: it already says *one rendering per value*, and a count is not a second rendering of the value any more than a length is a second rendering of a prompt. A binding with twelve permitted logins renders *12 users* on its row and the twelve logins in the editor, one click away, and nowhere else. *(This is the allow-list's own instance of the anti-duplication intent, stated as its own requirement rather than as another clause inside FR-051, so that a later change to FR-051 cannot silently change what a count may show.)*
- **FR-092** *(re-cut at v1.14.0 — the warning must know whether the binding can trigger at all, and must name only the triggers that are on; superseded wording recorded in changelog.md → `### v1.14.0`)*: **An absent policy is a worded warning everywhere a binding is listed — never a neutral row, and never a capability the binding does not currently have.** A binding whose `allowedUsers` is **absent** MUST render a **visible, worded warning** wherever the binding appears outside its own editor — the Bindings tab's row summary, and the Status tab's roll-up line (FR-093) — naming **who may start a session** and **naming the field that restricts it**. **Who** it names is **derived, never fixed**: the exposed surface of a binding with no allow-list is exactly the triggers switched on for it, so the warning MUST name only those, through FR-096's closed table. **Where** it may name them is narrower — the derivation belongs to surfaces that carry the binding's own state and switches, which is the Bindings row; the Status line is trigger-neutral by rule (FR-093, clarification row 48).
  The warning is additionally **state-aware**, and this is the half v1.11.0 left out. A binding that **cannot currently start a session** — because it is **disabled**, or because **no trigger is switched on** — MUST NOT be told, in the present tense, that anyone *can* start one. The same rule governs the **count** sentence a listed binding renders in place of the warning: `N users may trigger` is a capability claim and is subject to NFR-114 exactly as the warning is, which is why the table below carries both. The table is **closed** — three facts, eight rows — and every row's clause is normative:

  | Binding state | `allowedUsers` | Trigger switches | The row's policy clause |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | enabled | absent | one or more on | `open to anyone — anyone who can <derived> can start a session; name the logins who may in allowedUsers to change that` |
  | enabled | a list of N | one or more on | `N users may trigger` |
  | enabled | absent | **none on** | `nothing can start a session from this repository — no trigger is switched on; name the logins who may in allowedUsers` |
  | enabled | a list of N | **none on** | `no trigger is switched on, so nothing can start a session until one is; N users may trigger then` |
  | disabled | absent | one or more on | `nothing can start a session while this binding is off; when you enable it, anyone who can <derived> can start a session; name the logins who may in allowedUsers to change that` |
  | disabled | a list of N | one or more on | `N users may trigger once this binding is enabled` |
  | disabled | absent | **none on** | `nothing can start a session from this repository — it is off, and no trigger is switched on; name the logins who may in allowedUsers` |
  | disabled | a list of N | **none on** | `N users may trigger once this binding is enabled — though no trigger is switched on, so nothing can start a session yet` |

  `<derived>` is FR-096's clause for this binding's switches; `N` is the count FR-091 permits. Three clauses are **preserved verbatim from v1.11.0** and apply to every row of the table: the warning carries **text, never colour alone** (FR-083); it MUST NOT read as *protected*, *restricted*, *secure*, or *any user allowed* without the consequence attached (NFR-113); and it MUST NOT be phrased as an error — an absent policy is a **configuration the operator chose**, and a disabled binding is a configuration the operator chose twice over, so the panel's job is to make both legible, not to scold. **The counterfactual on a disabled binding is the point of the re-cut**, not a softening: the row leads with what *cannot* happen, then names what *would*, and the two are separated by an explicit conditional so the second can never be read as the first's continuation. A binding's own `state` member is already rendered as a row fact, so the disabled frame MUST NOT repeat the word *disabled* as a second state rendering (FR-039) — it is the conditional that does the work. This requirement remains the panel-side half of constitution II: the product fails closed on ambiguity, so the panel never leaves *"is this repository protected?"* **or** *"can this repository start a session right now?"* ambiguous.
- **FR-093** *(re-cut at v1.14.0 — the denominator is enabled bindings, and the consequence clause is trigger-neutral; superseded wording recorded in changelog.md → `### v1.14.0`)*: **Status states the count over enabled bindings and the consequence, names no login, and enumerates nothing.** The Status tab MUST state, from the per-binding `actorPolicy` member this document's `## Wire Surface Delta` Status row gains, **how many of the bindings it lists that are currently enabled carry no allow-list**, and MUST carry one line naming the consequence. **Both halves of the count are over enabled bindings only**, because a disabled binding can start no session and so is neither an exposure nor a non-exposure: it is excluded from the numerator **and** from the denominator, and the denominator MUST be **labelled** with the word *enabled* wherever a count renders, so the operator can see what is being counted. `StatusBindingView.active` (005 FR-032) is the member that decides this and it MUST be consulted — **a count that filters on `actorPolicy` alone is non-conforming, whatever it renders.**
  The consequence clause MUST be **trigger-neutral**: it states that the allow-list is the binding's only limit and that **whoever the trigger's own permission model lets act** can start a session. It MUST NOT name a trigger act — *open an issue*, *comment*, *assign*, *request a review* — because one clause spans N bindings whose switch sets differ and no single act is true of all of them. This is a **rule, not an omission**: per-binding derivation is the Bindings row's job (FR-096), and Status is forbidden from acquiring the data that would let it do that job instead (FR-039, clarification rows 42 and 48). The **five** shapes are **closed** — two unchanged, three re-cut:

  | Case | The line |
  | --- | --- |
  | Status unreadable | `Who may trigger: not available (service unreachable, or its status could not be read)` — unchanged |
  | **No** bindings at all | `Who may trigger: no bindings yet, so nothing can trigger until one is bound` — unchanged |
  | Bindings exist, **none enabled** | `Who may trigger: none of the N bindings is enabled, so nothing can start a session right now` |
  | Enabled bindings exist, **none of them open** | `Who may trigger: every enabled binding restricts who may trigger (M of M enabled); open the Bindings tab to see or change a binding's allowedUsers` |
  | Enabled bindings exist, **k of M open** | `Who may trigger: k of M enabled bindings has no allow-list, so whoever the trigger lets act can start a session; open the Bindings tab to see or change a binding's allowedUsers` |

  The **`0 of 0`** shape is **forbidden in every case**, including none-enabled. A bare `0 of 0` is the reassuring default NFR-113 exists to prevent: an operator reading it cannot tell a computed answer from a panel that did not check, and it is the one string on this tab that would be simultaneously uninformative and reassuring. A count of zero over a non-empty enabled set MUST render as the **positive statement** in its row above — never as an absent row, an empty line, or silence (FR-019, FR-003) — and the positive statement MUST be scoped to **enabled** bindings: `every binding restricts who may trigger` is **false** the moment a disabled binding is open, which is precisely the case that exposed the defect. The remaining v1.11.0 clauses are **preserved verbatim**: the line MUST NOT render any login; it **points at** the Bindings tab for the fix and **does not duplicate** the field or its editor (FR-039's no-duplication rule); and an unreadable status renders as **not available** with the service named, exactly like every other unmeasured value on that tab (NFR-112).
- **FR-094** *(re-cut at v1.13.0 — the basis clause asserted a falsehood about the provider; superseded wording recorded in changelog.md → `### v1.13.0`)*: **The dispatch row names the attributed actor, and states a basis only where the attribution's provenance needs one.** A dispatch row MUST render the **attributed actor's login** (002 FR-043). What it renders beside that login is a function of the attribution's basis, and the two cases are deliberately asymmetric:
  - **Basis `'direct'`** — GitHub named the identity that **performed the act**: the text's author for a mention, and the event's `assigner` or `review_requester` for an assignment or a review request (002 FR-044, FR-049, FR-050). **The row MUST render no basis clause at all.** There is nothing to qualify, and a qualifying sentence about a fact that is not in question is noise the operator pays to read on every row. This is now the **only** case a newly written row can reach.
  - **Basis `'subject-author'`** — **a legacy row, and it is rendered as one.** A row carrying this basis was written by an earlier build, under a rule that attributed assignment and review triggers to the issue or pull-request author because the **list** feeds named no actor. The row MUST render a clause saying the attribution was made **under the rule in force when the row was written** — naming the standing-in author and that the rule was the one then in force — so a reader can tell an inference from a record. The clause MUST NOT claim that GitHub fails to record the assigner or the reviewer: **GitHub does record both**, in `assigner` and `review_requester` (002 NFR-011 as re-cut, 002 FR-044), and the correction to that rule is 002 v1.12.0's. The member remains **readable** — this document may not refuse the row, because a dispatch row the panel cannot read hides an **entire** dispatch, which is a far worse lie than one clause (005 FR-019, NFR-112). **Reachable only by rows already on disk**: no row written under 002 v1.12.0 or later can carry this basis, so the clause is a compatibility rendering and not a live surface — stated explicitly so no future reader mistakes it for one and no operator is shown a second one.

  Every other clause of this requirement is **preserved verbatim**: each source reference in the reveal carries its **own** actor and basis, because a coalesced run can carry several (003 FR-011), and an actor who is **outside** the binding's policy but riding on an authorized run is thereby **visible** rather than silent. A run the gate refused renders 003's own label and reason for `blocked:actor-not-allowed` with the **denied login** named, and **Retry** is offered under FR-044's existing state table once the cause clears — the panel renders the service's verdict and never predicts it (FR-046). Every one of these strings renders through the non-HTML path (FR-080, NFR-101).
- **FR-095**: **A refusal the service returns splits back to the field, and never becomes a silent partial save.** A whole-file write the service refuses on the allow-list MUST render the service's **own** field-level refusal and remediation, MUST leave every other binding and every other field **byte-identical** (FR-058), MUST NOT report the value as saved, and MUST NOT echo the submitted value (FR-085). An **explicitly empty** list MUST be refused with the service's own wording and MUST NOT be rendered as *no one may trigger* — the two are different statements and only one of them is true. A save the panel cannot complete because the service is unreachable MUST leave the field showing the operator's own draft with the failure named and a retry offered (FR-019), never a reverted edit presented as a rejection by the service.
- **FR-096** *(added at v1.14.0 — the copy-derivation rule, stated as its own requirement so a later change to FR-092's tone cannot quietly change which acts are named)*: **Every rendering that says who may start a session derives that from the trigger switches that are actually on.** The exposed surface of a binding with no allow-list is exactly the set of triggers switched on for it, so a sentence naming any other act describes a surface the operator does not have — and an **overstated** exposure is not a safe default either: it is the alarm that teaches operators to stop reading the alarms (the changelog's *Why the register is preserved* note). One derivation, one closed table, one composition rule:

  | Trigger switch | Contributed phrase |
  | --- | --- |
  | `assignment` | `assign an issue to the account` |
  | `mention` | `mention the account in an issue or comment` |
  | `reviewRequest` | `request a review from the account on a pull request` |

  The switched-on switches' phrases are taken **in that fixed order** — `assignment`, `mention`, `reviewRequest`, matching the declared order of `PanelTriggers` and the wire's `BindingTriggers` — joined by `or`, and **the comma depends on the length of the list**: **exactly two phrases read `a or b` with no comma before `or`; three or more read `a, b, or c` with one** *(this length-dependent rule is the product-owner ruling at v1.15.0; see changelog.md → `### v1.15.0`)*. One phrase stands alone, because a list of one takes no separator at all. The result is placed inside the frame `anyone who can <phrases> can start a session`:

  | Switched on | Derived clause |
  | --- | --- |
  | all three | `anyone who can assign an issue to the account, mention the account in an issue or comment, or request a review from the account on a pull request can start a session` |
  | `mention` only | `anyone who can mention the account in an issue or comment can start a session` |
  | `assignment` only | `anyone who can assign an issue to the account can start a session` |
  | `reviewRequest` only | `anyone who can request a review from the account on a pull request can start a session` |
  | any two of three | those two phrases joined by ` or ` **with no comma** — a **composition** of the above, not a new rule |
  | **none** | **no clause at all** — there is no exposed surface, and FR-092's table supplies the sentence that says so |

  Four rules make the derivation total rather than merely present:

  1. **The input is always three booleans.** The derivation consumes `PanelTriggers`, which the panel's own reader completes from the MVP defaults for an absent flag (`readTriggerFlags`, mirroring `service/bindings.ts`'s parse-tolerant `triggersFieldOf`), so **no absent-switch case exists** and none needs a rendering. It MUST NOT invent one, and it MUST NOT treat an unreadable flag as on.
  2. **There is no fallback sentence.** Every one of the eight subsets maps to exactly one clause; a surface that cannot determine the switches renders **not available** for that clause under FR-019 and NFR-112, never a default wording. A fixed sentence reachable from any switch set is the defect this requirement exists to prevent.
  3. **The bound account's login is never named.** The phrases say *the account*, because the Bindings row already renders that member and this MUST NOT become a second rendering of it (FR-039, and FR-091's one-rendering intent). The account login is an **identity**, never a permitted login, so naming it here would not breach FR-091's count-only rule — it would still be duplication.
  4. **The register is preserved, not redesigned.** v1.11.0's sentence — *"anyone who can open an issue or comment on this repository can start a session"* — already had the frame this table fills; the derivation narrows **which acts** are named, never **how the sentence is built**. With only `mention` switched on, the derived clause *contains* v1.11.0's substance exactly; with `mention` off it is narrower, which is the correction.

  The derivation is consumed by the surfaces that carry the binding's own state and switches: the **Bindings row's** warning and count (FR-092's table), and the **editor guidance's** first state (FR-090's *no list configured* clause), in **either mode** — an add-mode draft's switches are the ones the editor is currently showing, and the guidance describes what would hold if that draft were saved, which is the only honest thing a field can say before it exists. It is **NOT** consumed by Status, which carries no trigger set and whose line is trigger-neutral by rule (FR-093, clarification row 48). Every string this requirement composes renders through the non-HTML path (FR-080, NFR-101).

#### K. The Status tab's refresh cadence (added at v1.16.0; GitHub issue #20)

The Status tab is the only surface in this product whose answer changes while nobody is acting, and it was the only one that never changed on its own. Three decisions were fixed before specification and are recorded as encoded: **Status tab only** — no other tab receives a cadence; **the period is the poll interval the service reports**, not a fixed 60 seconds; and **every activation reads immediately, while a repeating tick runs only while Status is the active tab**. The block's centre of gravity is one distinction this amendment had to make explicit rather than assume: **a refresh cadence is not a retry loop** (`specs/002-agent-event-extension/contracts/panel-service.md` §1).

- **FR-100**: **The Status tab re-reads `GET /v1/status` on a repeating cadence while it is the active tab, and re-reads on every activation. No other tab re-reads on a timer, and nothing anywhere retries a failed operation automatically.** Four obligations, each stated as behaviour.

  **1 · The tick's lifecycle.** The tick MUST be **armed when Status becomes the active tab** and MUST be **disarmed when any other tab is activated**, so a Status tab left in the background issues no reads — which is what makes the behaviour "while open" rather than "always". It MUST be **cleared on panel teardown**, as one of the timers FR-017 requires every tab's disposer to release, and it MUST NOT survive teardown in any state (NFR-108). There MUST be **at most one tick per panel** at any moment: arming Status twice, re-entering it repeatedly, or a teardown that raced an arming MUST NOT leave two timers or none. The period MUST be **the effective `polling.intervalMs` on the last status document the tab holds** — the number the Polling block already shows (FR-030, FR-039), which is the service's own default of 60 000 ms unless the operator configured otherwise within the documented 15 000–300 000 ms bounds. **When a later read reports a different interval, the armed period MUST follow it**; the tick is not pinned to the interval it was armed with.

  **2 · The in-flight read is respected, never stacked.** A tick MUST NOT begin a read while one is already in flight, and MUST NOT queue, defer, or chain one behind another. The existing in-flight guard is what enforces this — `loadStatus` returns early while `phase === 'loading'` (`src/status-tab.ts`), so a tick that fires during a slow or hung read is a **no-op that the next tick replaces**, not a second request. The guard MUST NOT be weakened or bypassed to make a tick "land": a missed tick MUST simply be missed, and the tab MUST NOT report a read it did not perform (FR-019). **The manual `Refresh status` button keeps the same guard** — pressing it during a read is refused rather than stacked, which is the same rule for both callers.

  **3 · What the interval is when it is not known — and the panel MUST NOT invent one.** The panel cannot know the cadence before its first status read returns, so **the tick cannot be armed at mount**: the mount-time read is the thing that supplies the interval, and the tick is armed from the document that read produced (FR-014's immediate-read-on-activation rule is what makes the window close). In the interval-less window the tab MUST render exactly as it does today — one read at activation, the stamp saying what it says, the `Refresh status` button present and live — and MUST NOT arm any tick. If **no interval is available at all** — the status document could not be read at all, **or it was refused because `polling.intervalMs` was unreadable**, which is a **failed read** and not a loaded document with a missing number (AGENTS invariant 8, FR-003), **or** the supplementary `GET /v1/config` read this tab already makes (FR-039) failed and supplied no interval either — then **no tick is armed, and the tab MUST say so in words**: that it is not refreshing itself, and that `Refresh status` reads on demand. This is a **read-only question, so the panel's answer is to render and say so, never to assume a number**: it MUST NOT fall back to 60 000 ms, to the service default, to any hard-coded period, or to the configured interval — an invented interval is NFR-112's defect in its purest form, because a panel that refreshes on a guessed period while the service polls on another is reporting on a machine that does not exist. **The `Refresh status` button remains fully available in every one of these states**, and the cadence statement is identical to the other one whenever no interval is available, whichever of the three ways reached it. **(re-cut at v1.16.0 — corrected in the same version; see changelog.md → `### v1.16.0`.)** The superseded clause read "…including after a read that failed and one that succeeded without an interval; a successful read that reports no interval is not a failed read and MUST NOT be rendered as one (FR-019)", which specified a state the fail-closed parser cannot produce: `polling.intervalMs` is a required member, so a document that omits or mistypes it refuses **whole** and the read is a failed one, subject to FR-019's ordinary handling.**

  **4 · This is a refresh cadence, not a retry loop — and the difference is what keeps 002's contract true.** A **cadence** re-reads a *successful operation* on a fixed period regardless of whether the last one succeeded; a **retry loop** re-attempts an operation that **failed**, escalating, backing off, or converging on success. `GET /v1/status` is a **read**: it has no outcome to retry, nothing to give up on, and nothing an operator has to press to finish. So the panel re-reading it once a minute is a cadence, and `specs/002-agent-event-extension/contracts/panel-service.md` §1's rule — `SERVICE_FAILED` maps to *`failed` health + manual retry button (**no automatic retry loop**)* — is **uncontradicted and unchanged**: a transport-level failure still resolves to a failed state with a button, never to a self-healing loop, and a tick that fails does exactly what a manual read does (FR-019: last document kept, marked stale, cause named, retry offered). The same holds for **006 AC-130 and AC-133's "no automatic retry"**, which govern **failed writes** — `503 storage-unavailable` refusals and unexpected failures carrying a correlation id — and are untouched by a read cadence on a different tab; this specification neither cites nor amends either. What the cadence MUST NOT become, in any circumstance: a backoff schedule, a doubling interval, a fast first retry after a failure, a shorter period while failing, or any behaviour that makes the panel's requests depend on how the last one went. **The period is a function of the reported interval and of nothing else** — not of the last read's outcome, not of the panel's own history, not of the operator's activity.

- **FR-101**: **The Status tab MUST show, in words, that it refreshes itself and when — and it MUST show the same thing whatever caused the read in front of the operator.** While a tick is armed, the tab MUST carry one statement of the fact and the period (for example *re-reads every 60 seconds* beside the Polling block's effective interval), and that statement MUST NOT change between a tick and a button press: the operator's own refresh and the automatic one are the same read at the same cadence, and a UI that treats them differently teaches an operator that the automatic one is a different, less trustworthy source. While **no** tick is armed (FR-100's interval-less window), the tab MUST instead state that it is **not** refreshing itself and name `Refresh status` as the way to read on demand — the honest value for an unknown cadence is *not refreshing*, never a silent static page (NFR-112). A statement about the cadence is **text, never colour alone** (FR-083), and MUST render through the non-HTML path (FR-080).

#### L. The zero-account binding gate, and the reason copy that must be true either way (added at v1.17.0; GitHub issue #18)

`repaintBindingActions` gates *New binding* on the read state alone, so with **zero accounts** the control stays **enabled**, the add form opens onto an account picker that is **already disabled** — there is nothing to pick, and the field's placeholder still asks for a *verified* account, a state the product does not have — and the submission is refused with *"Pick the account this repository polls under."* The operator has to type a repository and pick a project to reach a dead end. The list's own empty text compounds it: *"No binding yet — select New binding to add one, or refresh."* instructs the operator to press the control this block disables. This is the same defect class v1.13.0 and v1.14.0 removed one layer along — **a string asserting something the machine does not support** — arriving from the *control* rather than from the *copy*: the control claims a capability it has not got, and four strings then describe a path that does not exist.

**Four facts decide this block, and they are the whole of it.** The gate is about **accounts at all**; the reason is **text**, on the surface that carries the control it explains; **Accounts is named, nothing navigates there, and every string names a state the product actually has**; and the one case the gate deliberately does **not** cover gets its own honest sentence rather than silence. **Two frames exist only before the accounts read has succeeded** — at mount, in flight, and after a failed read — and this block answers them **in copy, never by starting the gate early**: FR-120 bars the gate there by name, FR-121's line stays absent because an unread list is not evidence of an absence, and FR-122's table carries a **third row** so the empty text names only *Refresh* — FR-120's accounts gate never disables it, though the in-flight window does at `loading` (`005 AC-157`) (clarification row 60).
**v1.17.0 changes no wire and no service, and that MUST NOT be re-opened here.** `service/routes/bindings.ts` builds `hasAccount` from the custody directory and `service/bindings.ts` validates only that the referenced account **exists** — never its `state` across `pending_handoff`, `verifying`, `active`, `rejected`, `revoked`, `error` (FR-062). That permissiveness is **correct**, because **a binding is meant to outlive its account's health**: an account mid-connect is a legitimate write-time target, `error` / `revoked` / `rejected` are legitimate targets too, and a dead token must not force the operator to delete and rebuild a binding. The poll loop already carries the consequence — it skips such a binding as `'inactive-account'` — and FR-063 renders that on the row. **Requiring an `active` account at write time would be a product regression**, and the gate below is a **panel gate on a control**, never a service rule.

- **FR-120**: **The gate is zero accounts *at all*.** *New binding* MUST be disabled whenever the panel's successfully read account list is **empty** — no account in any lifecycle state — in addition to the read-state condition already gating it. This condition MUST be exactly *"no accounts exist"*. It MUST NOT widen to *"no usable account exists"*, and the distinction is deliberate rather than incidental: the panel's per-account predicate `usable` is true of the `active` state **alone**, so a widened gate would lock the operator out of binding to the accounts the service accepts and the poll loop still tracks — converting a deliberate service permissiveness into a panel prohibition and producing bindings the operator cannot create and cannot delete. The gate MUST key on a **successfully read, empty** list and MUST NOT fire on a **failed or not-yet-started** read: an unreadable account list is FR-019's failed read, which renders its cause and a retry, and the absence of an answer MUST NOT be presented as the absence of accounts. This bar is **load-bearing and is not weakened by FR-122's third row** — where the gate cannot be evaluated the *empty text* answers truthfully rather than the gate starting to fire, because a pre-read gate would assert an absence from evidence that has not arrived (clarification row 53). The disabled attribute is necessary but **not sufficient** to convey the reason — FR-121 is the obligation that carries it (FR-083, NFR-112).
- **FR-121**: **The reason is one worded line under the list's toolbar, and it names Accounts without navigating there.** While the gate holds, the Bindings list block MUST render one line, **positioned under the list's control row** — beside the control it explains, rather than inside the editor the operator has not been able to open — carrying **text** and never the disabled attribute or colour alone (FR-083, NFR-112). The string is **closed**:

  | Case | The line |
  | --- | --- |
  | The account list is empty — FR-120's gate holds | `Add an account on the Accounts tab before binding a repository.` |

  The line MUST **name the Accounts tab**, and it MUST NOT be phrased as an error or as a fault the operator committed: an operator with no accounts has misconfigured nothing, and this is the same tone obligation FR-092 already states for an absent allow-list, reused here. It MUST **point at** the Accounts tab and MUST NOT **duplicate** that tab's guidance, its add flow, or its controls — the *points at, does not duplicate* rule FR-038 applies to the project-picker guidance and FR-093 applies to Status's line (FR-039). **No button, link, or any other control is added** — not to the Bindings toolbar, not to the list, not anywhere on the tab — to carry or to act on the reason; text is the whole delivery. It MUST NOT occupy or overwrite the tab's existing **action note**, the line that reports what an action *did* — a refused write, a removal, a failed read — which has obligations of its own (FR-019, FR-085). The reason is a **distinct channel with a distinct obligation**, and neither may be rendered where the other belongs. When the gate does not hold the line MUST be **absent**: an always-present line that is routinely blank is the reassuring absence NFR-112 exists to prevent, and it would also occupy the note channel's space for a state it has nothing to say about. Every string renders through the non-HTML path (FR-080).
- **FR-122**: **The empty-list text MUST NOT name a control the operator cannot press.** The list's empty message and FR-121's line arise from the **same state** and therefore MUST move together: the empty message exists to tell the operator how to get a first row, and *New binding* is the only route to one. The strings are **closed**:

  | Case | The list's empty text |
  | --- | --- |
  | The account list is empty — FR-120's gate holds | `No binding yet — add an account on the Accounts tab first.` |
  | At least one account exists — the gate does not hold | `No binding yet — select New binding to add one, or refresh.` — **unchanged**, because the control it names is live |
  | **The accounts read has not succeeded** — not yet started, in flight, or **failed** — so FR-120's gate cannot hold and *New binding* is disabled by the read-state condition alone | `No binding yet — the account list is not known. Refresh to read it.` |

  **The consistency rule, stated so that the property is structural rather than promised.** Every row of this table is selected by **one predicate and nothing else** — *whether the accounts read has succeeded* — which has exactly **three** outcomes and therefore **three** rows:

  | The predicate | FR-120's gate | *New binding* | This requirement's row |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | The read has **not** succeeded | cannot hold — barred by name | disabled, by the read-state condition | the **not-known** row |
  | The read has succeeded, the list is **empty** | **holds** | disabled | the *add an account* row |
  | The read has succeeded, the list holds **≥ 1** | does not hold | **enabled** | the *select New binding* row |

  **No row names a control that is unavailable in the state its own row describes**, and the three rows partition the predicate, so **no fourth row exists to hold an unwritten case**. The structural consequence is the property that matters: the gate is itself a **conjunction over this same read-success fact** (FR-120 forbids it firing unless the read succeeded *and* the list is empty), so the row and the control's state are **derived from one fact and cannot disagree**. Whenever *New binding* is disabled, the text names *Refresh* or the Accounts tab and never *New binding*; whenever the text names *New binding*, the read succeeded with at least one account and the control is live. **The third row's selector is the read-success conjunct alone — never `accounts.length`.** That is load-bearing: `loadBindings` deliberately **retains the previous account list** when a read fails, so a failed read that followed a successful read of an **empty** list leaves `accounts.length === 0` — and a length-keyed empty text would render the *add an account* row over an unanswered read, asserting an absence it has not established. The read-success conjunct is what prevents that.

  **The third row states an absence of knowledge, never an absence of accounts.** It MUST NOT say or imply that the operator has **no** accounts, and it MUST NOT name *New binding*; the only control it may name is **Refresh**. **Which gates Refresh is a distinction this row depends on, so it is stated rather than assumed.** FR-120's accounts **gate** never disables Refresh, and it is not what this row's sentence rests on. The **in-flight read window** does: Refresh is unavailable while a read is `loading`, and available again at `idle` and after a **failed** read, which re-enables it. The row's advice is therefore **actionable** exactly where the row's purpose lies — telling the operator the account list is not known and naming the control that reads it — which is at `idle` before the first read and after a **failed** one, because a failed read both leaves the list unknown and restores the control. At `loading` the advice is **momentarily** unavailable, while the read the operator would start is already in flight and about to answer the very question the row raises. **That overlap is accepted and bounded, not an oversight**: it is one render of a state that resolves on its own, and the alternative — ungating Refresh to erase it — is a **read-behaviour change belonging to its own requirement**, not something a copy decision may do. **This requirement MUST NOT be satisfied by ungating Refresh**, and MUST NOT move Refresh off the read-state condition; a double read during flight introduced here would be scope creep in the costume of a fix. It MUST NOT restate the failed read's cause or its retry: those belong to the tab's own failed-read channel, which renders them (FR-019), and FR-121's channel rule applies here too — the empty text is not where a read's outcome is reported. Where the accounts read has not succeeded **and** there is nothing to say about bindings beyond their absence, this row is the whole of the truth available, and a not-yet-read or failed list is **not evidence of an absence**.
  **This row does not weaken FR-120.** FR-120's bar on the gate firing before the read has succeeded is **untouched**, and the reason line of FR-121 stays **absent** in this frame, because a list the panel could not read says nothing about whether an account exists. What changes here is only **which honest sentence the empty text carries while the gate cannot be evaluated** — the gate does not start firing, the copy starts telling the truth.

  Neither **empty-message** string is the other, and they MUST NOT be collapsed into one: the empty message describes an **empty list**, FR-121's line describes a **blocked control**, and writing one sentence to serve both would lose the fact that the list is empty while saying what to do about it. This requirement governs the **empty list only** — a **non-empty** list renders its rows and none of these strings appears, because neither the emptiness nor the gate is what the operator is looking at then. No row may use *select*, *click*, *press*, or *choose* for a control the operator cannot currently use, and no row may render at all while the control it names is available and the list is not empty.
- **FR-123**: **Accounts that exist but none of which is `active` is a second case, and the copy says so.** This block deliberately does **not** gate it, because the picker is **right** to be empty: the operator's problem is a credential to fix or replace, not a repository to bind. With accounts present and none usable, *New binding* therefore **stays enabled** and the add form opens onto an empty, disabled picker. What MUST change is the **refusal** the add form produces when no account is selected — today *"Pick the account this repository polls under."*, which tells the operator to choose from a list that has nothing in it. The refusal is **closed and total**, one line per reachable case. *`active`* is the panel's own `usable` predicate, true of the `active` lifecycle state alone (FR-062's six states):

  | Accounts in the panel's list | The refusal |
  | --- | --- |
  | **None exist** | `Add an account on the Accounts tab before binding a repository.` — reachable only when the list emptied while the editor was already open, since the gate otherwise prevents opening it. It MUST be **FR-121's own string, the same constant rendered in a second position**, not a separately worded copy, because it is the **same reason** and two spellings of one reason are two things that can drift (FR-091's one-rendering intent) |
  | Some exist, **none is `active`** | `No active account — fix or replace an account on the Accounts tab.` |
  | Some exist, **at least one is `active`**, none selected | `Pick the account this repository polls under.` — **unchanged**, and actionable, because there is something to pick |

  The no-active-account refusal MUST name the **fix or replace** remediation instead of a selection the operator cannot make, MUST carry **text, not colour alone** (FR-083), and MUST NOT echo anything submitted (FR-085). It stays in the add form's existing note channel, because that is where the operator is when they press *Add binding* and the only place they learn which fix applies. FR-121's persistent toolbar line MUST **not** appear in this case, because the tab is **not** blocked — a binding becomes addable the moment an account reaches `active`, and a standing notice about a condition the operator is one fix from clearing is a nag, which is the objection clarification row 41 settled for the other warning on this tab.

  **The picker field's placeholder is the third string this requirement fixes, and it is the one that named a state the product does not have.** The picker lists exactly the accounts the panel marks `usable` — the `active` lifecycle state **alone** — so its placeholder MUST name that state and nothing else. It is a **closed string, and it does not vary by case**:

  | Field | The string |
  | --- | --- |
  | The account picker's placeholder, whenever the picker is empty | `Select an active account` |

  **`Select a verified account` is withdrawn.** *Verified* is not one of `AccountState`'s six values (`pending_handoff`, `verifying`, `active`, `rejected`, `revoked`, `error` — FR-062), so the product has no state by that name; the picker holds only accounts that are `active`, which makes *active* the product's own word for what the list contains. This is the **same obligation the refusal above states**, applied to the one string that broke it: **a rendered string MUST NOT name a state the product does not have.** The rule governs the placeholder's **wording, never its presence**: the picker MUST still be disabled whenever its option list is empty, and the **visible refusal or reason stays the obligation that tells the operator which fix applies** (FR-121, and this requirement's refusal table). The placeholder is the field's own hint and MUST NOT be treated as discharging that obligation — a field that asks for a *verified* account beside a refusal saying there is *no active* one states two different facts about one list. Every string renders through the non-HTML path (FR-080).
- **FR-124**: **This is panel-side only, and the `usable`-versus-`exists` divergence is out of scope.** No requirement in this block changes a wire member, a route, a status code, an error code, `PUT /v1/bindings`'s validation, a stored document, a `host.storage` key, or any contract under `specs/002-agent-event-extension/contracts/`; `GET /v1/bindings` and `GET /v1/accounts` are read exactly as they are today (FR-025, FR-050, NFR-106). The service's **exists-not-state** validation is **correct and is not reopened** — the preamble states why, and the `## Wire Surface Delta` Bindings row records that nothing on it moves. **Known, deliberately unaddressed divergence, recorded so it is not rediscovered as a bug:** the panel's account picker filters on `usable` — the account being `active` — while the service's `hasAccount` filters on **existence**. The two answer different questions and **both are right for their own question**; making them agree would mean either refusing bindings the service accepts, which is the product regression above, or offering accounts the poll loop will skip as `'inactive-account'` — a consequence FR-063 already renders honestly on each affected row and one the operator can act on from there. Re-opening this is **scope creep** for GitHub issue #18. It is named here as a **decision**, not a backlog item this block waits on.

### Key Entities

- **Tab**: one of the six named top-level surfaces. Attributes: id, label, accessible name, whether its body is mounted, when it was last read, its read state. Exactly one is active at a time; each is reachable by keyboard; none is reachable a second way. **Status is the one tab that owns a repeating read**, armed while it is active and disarmed when it is not; the other five read only on activation that reaches them for the first time or on an explicit operator action (FR-014, FR-100).
- **Read cadence**: the fixed period on which the Status tab re-reads `GET /v1/status` while it is the active tab. **Not an entity the panel holds a default for** — it is the effective `polling.intervalMs` from the last status document the tab holds, and it is *unknown* until that document arrives, which is a state the tab renders (FR-100). Distinct from a retry: a cadence repeats a read, a retry repeats a **failed operation**, and this product automates only the former (FR-019, FR-100).
- **Last-read stamp**: the time of the tab's last **successful** read, rendered on every tab. Asserts **one** thing — how old the data on screen is — and can no longer distinguish a manual refresh from a tick, so nothing renders it as if it could (FR-014).
- **Status projection**: the operator's answer to *is it working*. Carries service health and uptime, the data directory, schema version, storage writability, per-account identity, connection state and rate budget, per-binding last scan and pending count, the polling cadence with its next-poll stamp and honest paused reason, and the agent pin's verification status. **The one entity 005 materially changes**: three of its members are hardcoded literals today.
- **Dispatch row**: one unit of work as the operator reads it — subject, repository, trigger, state with its reason, attempt, target project, worktree option, session reference, verification outcome, source references, correlation identifier. Internally a **run** (FR-022); the two terms are related by the mapping table and are not interchangeable in identifiers.
- **Affordance**: a state-dependent action available on a dispatch row — *Retry*, *Resolve*, *Return to waiting*, *Open source* — derived from the state by a single table. Absence is meaningful and carries its reason.
- **Binding**: a repository watched under an account, routed to a project, with a trigger set, a worktree option, a starting prompt, and an **optional allow-list of GitHub logins permitted to trigger it** (002 FR-047). Already correctly named; unchanged in shape except by that one additive member; rendered with its scan state, its allow-list **count**, and — when it has none — a worded warning (FR-092). **As of v1.14.0 the policy rendering is a function of three members rather than of the allow-list alone**: the row states what *this* binding can start a session on, given its own `state` and its own trigger switches, because a binding that is off or that watches nothing has no exposed surface to describe (FR-092's table, FR-096, NFR-114).
- **Account**: a durable GitHub identity under service custody. Carries lifecycle state, connection state, scope check, verification stamp, and an optional display name. **Credential-free by construction**; the credential exists only in the account file at rest.
- **Prerequisite**: a setup condition the operator must satisfy, each with its own state — met, not met, or **not checkable by the panel** — and its own remediation.
- **Diagnostic**: a read-only record the panel kept about its own operation — a ledger entry, the evidence schema version, an observed phase. Shown, never editable (FR-075); **confirmed 2026-09-28** as surviving in About, with only the live *Record phase* writer dropped (FR-011, `## Resolved Gate Questions` entry 2).

## Non-Functional Requirements

- **NFR-101 Rendering safety.** Every string this feature adds to the panel renders through the existing non-HTML path (FR-080, 003 NFR-109). Verified by a rendering test per tab and by the existing bundle assertions. The new operator-authored surfaces — issue titles, repository names, account display names, starting prompts — are covered explicitly.
- **NFR-102 Secret containment.** Zero credential occurrences in any panel state, any `host.storage` value, any rendered surface, any audit row, any DTO, or either committed bundle (002 FR-007, 003 NFR-106, 004 NFR-121). Extended with: the display name is refused on a credential shape (FR-066), and the secret-scan suites gain cases for the Accounts tab rather than exemptions.
- **NFR-103 Durability and preservation.** The upgrade loses nothing (FR-005): no binding, account, checkpoint, delivery, run, or audit row is quarantined, reset, or rewritten, and the pre-003 queue vocabulary continues to project through 003's migration table. Verified by a test that writes rows in the retired vocabulary and asserts they render and remain dispatchable after the upgrade.
- **NFR-104 Idempotency of read** *(clause re-cut at v1.16.0 — GitHub issue #20; the rule's target was never "a read", it was a **side effect*)*. Opening a tab, switching away and back, or switching back to the active tab MUST NOT mutate any state and MUST NOT **change anything because it was looked at** (FR-014). On the five tabs that are not Status, the tab MUST NOT re-read unless the operator asks. On **Status** it MAY re-read — on every activation and on the reported cadence (FR-100) — and MUST NOT do anything else differently. A panel that dispatches because it was looked at is a panel that cannot be looked at; the same sentence is why an activation-triggered read is safe here, because `GET /v1/status` is a read and a read mutates nothing (FR-002, FR-089).
- **NFR-105 Offline determinism.** Everything this feature adds is testable with no live host, no real token, and no network (FR-086). The status projection is testable against a temp-directory store with a driven clock; pagination is testable against fixture pages; the vocabulary is testable by asserting no user-facing string contains a retired term.
- **NFR-106 Compatibility.** Unchanged SDK pin, unchanged engine floor (`>=1.24.0`), no new host capability, API, or permission, no undocumented or private interface (003 NFR-110, FR-004, FR-079).
- **NFR-107 Accessibility.** The six tabs and every control in them are operable by keyboard alone, expose accessible names, associate each body with its tab, convey state as text as well as colour, and remain usable at the narrowest supported frame width (FR-016, FR-081, FR-082, FR-083).
- **NFR-108 Teardown completeness.** After teardown there is no orphan node, no surviving timer, no retained runtime, and no remaining host subscription, verified by a test that counts nodes and disposers before and after (FR-017, FR-018). **The Status tab's refresh tick is one of the timers this counts** (v1.16.0): it MUST be among those released, and the count MUST return to its pre-mount value whether the panel was torn down on Status or on another tab (FR-100).
- **NFR-109 Code quality.** Zero suppressions, zero `any`, one dispatch path, one runtime, one set of validators (FR-088, `AGENTS.md` invariant 7).
- **NFR-110 Release discipline.** Both bundles rebuilt and committed with their sources; `SERVICE_VERSION` pinned to `package.json` by test; the version remains pre-1.0.0 and is not bumped incidentally (FR-087, `AGENTS.md` invariants 1, 2, 5).
- **NFR-111 Observability.** A failed read on any tab is visible with its cause and a retry affordance, and stale content is marked stale rather than silently retained (FR-019). A panel that cannot answer must say that it cannot answer. **A repeated read is no exception** (v1.16.0): a tick's failure is rendered exactly as a button press's failure, the last-read stamp keeps describing the data on screen, and a cadence that cannot arm says it is not refreshing rather than rendering a page that silently never changes (FR-014, FR-100, FR-101).
- **NFR-112 Honest defaults.** No rendered value in any of the six tabs is a plausible-looking stand-in for an unmeasured one: unmeasured is unmeasured, unknown is unknown, not checkable is not checkable, and not available is not available (FR-003, FR-033, FR-034, FR-072). This is the single NFR against which the feature should be reviewed. **An unreadable refresh cadence is *not refreshing*** (v1.16.0): before the panel has read an interval it MUST NOT display a period, and it MUST NOT arm a tick on a number it did not read (FR-100, FR-101). A 60-second number that appears before any document has arrived is the same defect NFR-113 exists to prevent, pointed at the clock instead of at a security control — plausible, reassuring, and wrong.
- **NFR-113 No implied policy** (added at v1.11.0): no rendered element on any of the six tabs may present a binding as **protected**, **restricted**, or **secure** unless the service has actually said its `actorPolicy` is `restricted`; and no element may present an **absent** policy as a neutral or healthy value. An absent policy renders as a worded warning with its consequence named (FR-092), a count of zero renders as a positive statement rather than as absence (FR-093), and an unreadable status renders as *not available* (FR-093, NFR-112). This is NFR-112 aimed at the one value in the product where a plausible-looking default would be **a security control that does not exist**: every other unmeasured value misleads about effort or cost, and this one would mislead about who can start sessions in the operator's own repositories.
- **NFR-114 No unearned capability** (added at v1.14.0): no string the panel renders about a binding may assert, unframed and in the present tense, a capability the binding does not currently have. A binding that is **disabled**, or that has **no trigger switched on**, can start no session at all, so every rendering about one — the row's warning, the row's **count**, the editor's guidance, the Status line — MUST either say nothing about triggering or state what would hold once the binding is enabled and a trigger is on. **Counting is not asserting**: `N users may trigger` remains permitted on a binding that can trigger (FR-091) and is barred on one that cannot. This is **NFR-113 pointed at exposure rather than at authorization**: NFR-113 forbids implying a control the service has not reported, and this forbids implying an exposure the machine does not have. They are the same defect — a sentence the product cannot support — approached from opposite ends, and a copy sweep that fixed only one would leave the other live. The sweep that verifies this runs over **every** rendered string about a binding rather than only over FR-092's warning, because the count sentence is where the defect was found (clarification row 45).

- **005 AC-152** *(a failing tick is a failing read, and the period does not chase the failure — added at v1.16.0)*: Given three consecutive ticks whose status read is refused, then the last successfully read document is still on screen marked **stale**, the exact cause is named, there is **one** stale marker rather than one per tick, the last-read stamp is unchanged, `Refresh status` is enabled and issues a read on demand, and the fourth period is armed at **the same interval as the first** — asserted by the request log, which contains no request earlier than one full interval after the previous one. Given `idle`, and again given a **failed** read, the row names Refresh **and Refresh is enabled**, so the advice the row gives is one the operator can take in the state the row renders — which is the whole of the row's purpose, since a failed read both leaves the list unknown and restores the control. Given `loading`, then the row still renders and Refresh is **disabled**, and this is the **accepted, bounded** overlap FR-122 states rather than an unasserted defect: the criterion asserts the overlap **as it is** so that a later change to either half is visible, and asserts **no** requirement text claims otherwise. Given **any** read state, then FR-120's accounts **gate** has **not** disabled Refresh — asserted by the same fixture set, because a gate that spread to the Refresh control would be a second gate this amendment never specified. **The criterion also pins the rejected alternative as a failure**: an implementation that satisfies the third row by **ungating Refresh**, or by moving Refresh off the read-state condition, fails this criterion on the predicate alone — and the assertion is written as equality with `status === 'loading'` precisely so that *always enabled* cannot pass as *not gated by the accounts read*.

- **Renaming wire route paths** — `/v1/events*` → `/v1/dispatches*` and the `repositories` status member → `bindings`. Deferred with the reason in FR-023 and **confirmed deferred by the product owner on 2026-09-28** (Gate Question 1, `## Resolved Gate Questions` entry 1). A settled deferral, not an open item: the retained paths are correct as they stand, and a future rename is a wire-contract change taken up deliberately by whoever makes it.
- **Settings editing, saving, live-apply, and destructive-knob confirmation** (feature 006). The tab is read-only here (FR-070).
- **The per-binding `PATCH /v1/bindings/:bindingId` state machine** and its draft/project-missing states, which 002 records as deliberate MVP debt. 005 uses the whole-file grant (FR-050).
- **Any GitHub write**, in any role, including as a way to resolve or acknowledge a dispatch (002 FR-031, FR-002).
- **The service-side `agentVerified` mirror** — backlog. Status reports its absence honestly rather than inventing it (FR-033).
- **Service-side retention, export, and restore** of configuration or audit data — backlog.
- **Durable deduplication-index eviction** — backlog, named in 003's edge cases.
- **Policy profiles**, per-action autonomy toggles with real gates, approval workflows, and the `waiting_approval` state. 002 FR-027's gates remain documentation-only.
- **Promoting agent verification from warn-only to blocking** — 003 records 002's v1.1.0 deviation as standing (FR-047).
- **Work-completion tracking.** No signal tells the service an agent finished, so a `dispatched` run stays `dispatched` and no tab may imply otherwise.
- **Automatic cleanup** of sessions, worktrees, or projects — manual only (002 FR-040, FR-077).
- **Any new host capability, API, or permission** (NFR-106, FR-004).
- **Multi-instance or cross-machine behaviour**, multi-tenancy, webhook ingress, other providers, Docker packaging, hosted operation.
- **A mobile or narrow-native panel layout.** The panel remains an extension rail; it must be usable at the narrowest width the host allows, not designed for one.
- **Phase 4 plan, research, data model, contracts, and task breakdown, and Phase 6 implementation** — those are the architect's phases.
- **A GitHub identity picker, search, or typeahead for the allow-list** (v1.11.0). The host exposes no such API and this document may not invent one (FR-004); the field is free text the service validates. A future adapter may add one without changing the field's stored shape.
- **Showing the permitted logins anywhere outside the editor field** (v1.11.0) — FR-091. Count on a row, never text.
- **Reopening the per-binding `PATCH /v1/bindings/:bindingId` MVP debt** (v1.11.0). `allowedUsers` rides the whole-file grant, exactly as `startingPrompt` did.
- **A global or account-level allow-list, a shared/reusable list object, or a team- or role-based rule** (v1.11.0). A flat list of logins on one binding is the whole model; anything more needs its own feature and its own identity model.
- **Any panel-side policy check** (v1.11.0). The service decides (003 FR-076); the panel renders the verdict. A pre-check in the panel would be a second implementation of the rule.
- **Any migration or upgrade path for a binding stored without `allowedUsers`** (v1.11.0). Nothing has been released and the product owner ruled it: *"there are no migrations needed as we haven't released yet."* Absent is the specified state.
- **Any wire, service, or store change** (v1.17.0). The zero-account gate is a panel gate on a control: no route, wire member, status or error code, `PUT /v1/bindings` validation, stored document, or contract under `specs/002-agent-event-extension/contracts/` moves (FR-124).
- **Requiring an `active` account at write time**, or narrowing `PUT /v1/bindings` to accept only `active` accounts (v1.17.0). Rejected on purpose, not overlooked: a binding is meant to **outlive its account's health**, a dead token must not force the operator to delete and rebuild a binding, and the poll loop already carries the consequence as `'inactive-account'` (FR-063, FR-124).
- **Re-opening the panel's `usable`-versus-`exists` divergence** — the picker filters on the account being `active`, the service filters on the account existing (v1.17.0). The two answer different questions, both are right for their own, and reconciling them in issue #18 would be scope creep. Recorded as a decision in FR-124 and in the changelog, not as a backlog item.
- **A navigation control from Bindings to Accounts** — a button, link, or tab-switch affordance carrying FR-121's reason (v1.17.0). The owner's decision was **text**, and text is the whole delivery; a button here would be a second route to a capability and would have to be kept in step (FR-010, FR-039).
- **Ungating Refresh, or moving it off the read-state condition** (v1.17.0). FR-122's third row names Refresh and names it correctly; the momentary unavailability during an in-flight read is **accepted and bounded**, and removing it would be a **read-behaviour change** — a double read during flight — belonging to its own requirement, not to a copy decision. FR-122 states that it MUST NOT be satisfied this way, and `005 AC-157` fails an implementation that does it (clarification row 61).
- **The Status tab's per-binding count under the zero-account condition — a second recorded residual** (v1.17.0). With **zero accounts**, the Status tab still renders *Bindings active / N binding(s) active* while the Bindings tab renders *Accounts: 0* and gates *New binding*. **The two surfaces disagree about the same store**: Status counts stored bindings and reports them as *active*, while Bindings reports that the panel has no account through which any binding could poll. Nothing can poll with no account, so Status's word **overstates capability** — the same defect class this amendment exists to eliminate, **one surface up**: a rendered value asserting something the machine does not support, which is what NFR-112 and FR-003 exist to prevent. **Deliberately unaddressed here, and recorded rather than fixed**, for two reasons: **issue #18 is a Bindings-tab fix** and its approved scope is FR-120 – FR-124 plus the copy they govern, so changing the Status projection here would be scope creep against a requirement set that has just been renumbered; and **main's v1.16.0 (PR #28, GitHub issue #20) has just rewritten the Status projection** — its own block K re-cut FR-030, FR-039, FR-014 and FR-019 around the refresh cadence — so a fix written against the pre-#28 projection would land on moved text. **The correction belongs to whichever change next touches the Status projection**, and it must treat *active* as a function of an account being able to poll rather than as `BindingRecord.active` alone. **No Status-tab behaviour, code, requirement, or test changes in this amendment**, and the residual is **not** a backlog item this amendment waits on — it is a decision, recorded in FR-124's spirit and here.
## Assumptions

Each assumption below is a documented default chosen where the requirement text and the predecessors' text were silent. Each is reversible without a redesign, and each is a candidate for the gate.

- **Status is the landing tab** (FR-015). Chosen because the operator's first question is whether the machine is working, and a panel that opens on a configuration surface answers a question nobody asked yet. **It is also the only tab that changes on its own** (FR-100, GitHub issue #20): the landing tab is the one an operator leaves open while something is happening, so it is the one whose answer must not freeze at the moment they looked at it.
- **The active tab is not persisted.** `host.storage` already hosts last-viewed UI state (002 FR-034); this feature declines to use it for the active tab. Reversible in one line if the owner prefers persistence, and no key is added or removed either way (FR-015, FR-025).
- **Tab bodies mount lazily on first activation** (FR-013). Chosen because the host clears subscriptions on unmount, pause, remove, and server switch, so eager mounting would open six read paths at panel open.
- **A failed read keeps the last successful content, visibly marked stale** (FR-019), rather than blanking. Chosen because an operator watching a binding's last scan prefers "this is 40 minutes old" to nothing.
- **The relay loop is owned by the Dispatches tab.** Recorded as an assumption rather than a requirement's conclusion because ownership is an implementation decision the plan makes; FR-018 fixes the *constraint* (exactly one loop, unaffected by tab switching) and this is the default that satisfies it. Moving ownership to the panel root is equally conforming.
- **Default page size 25**, selectable 10/25/50/100 (FR-042). Chosen because 25 rows is roughly one screen of the rail and roughly one polling interval of activity.
- **Filters are by binding and by state**, applied server-side (FR-043). Chosen because a client-side filter over a paged read would filter a page, not a history, and would lie about its own result. **Confirmed by the product owner 2026-09-28** (`## Resolved Gate Questions` entry 3).
- **Diagnostics survives as a read-only section in About** (FR-075), with only the live *Record phase* writer dropped (FR-011). Chosen as the default because the evidence schema version and the ledger are compatibility surfaces (`AGENTS.md` invariant 10) and removing the only reader of them leaves nothing to notice a version bump. **Confirmed by the product owner 2026-09-28** (`## Resolved Gate Questions` entry 2).
- **`displayName` is a new durable field on the account** with a `null` default (FR-066). Chosen because today a GitHub login rename overwrites the only label the operator sees, which is a small but real loss of operator intent. **Confirmed by the product owner 2026-09-28** (`## Resolved Gate Questions` entry 4).
- **Pre-1.0.0 stays.** No tab, rename, or route change in this feature is a reason to bump `version`, and `1.0.0` remains a product-owner decision (`AGENTS.md` invariant 2, FR-087).
- **The prerequisites surface stays read-only**, reporting state and remediation and never offering to change the host's Default Agent setting, which the extension cannot read or write.
- **The refresh cadence's period is the effective `polling.intervalMs`, and the panel holds no default for it** (FR-100). Chosen because the panel and the service poll the same loop on the same period: a cadence the panel chose would be a second number that can disagree with the one the operator is shown on the Polling block. The cost is the interval-less window before the first read returns, which is specified as render-and-declare rather than filled with a guess (FR-101, clarification row 52). **Reversible in one line** if the owner prefers a fixed panel period — but it would then be a number the service never reported.
- **Scale**: fewer than ten bound repositories, a handful of accounts, one local OpenChamber installation, one logical service instance, one operator machine. Concurrency correctness is required across panels, not across service instances.
- **Provider**: GitHub.com REST API; GitHub Enterprise compatibility is unchanged and still requires later review.
