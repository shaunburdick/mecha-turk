### v1.0.0 — 2026-09-28 (extension amendment; feature 005 adds the panel information architecture)

- **Rationale**: 005 is the surface the previous three specifications were written to enable. 003 built honest retry and a state model and said the restructuring "lands in 005"; 004 added a field and said the Bindings tab renders it "exactly once"; 002's health requirement (FR-036) has had a status document to render into since before either existed, and it is being rendered into a projection with four hardcoded members. Leaving 005 out of the documents that own those facts would make four specs describe four different panels.
- **Sequencing decision**: 005 **extends** all three predecessors and **supersedes** exactly one row — 003's `## Wire Surface Delta` entry for `Status`, which 003 marked "Unchanged" and 005 changes. 005 is also **unimplemented**, so this is a **record amendment**, exactly as 003's v1.1.0 was for 004: there is no deployed state to invalidate and no gap to defer, only a design that is free to be made around the delta while it is still free.
- **Principles reviewed, unchanged in substance**: Principle I (no secret in any store, log, or rendered surface) is served by the Accounts tab's credential-free DTO and the refusal of credential-shaped display names. Principle II (safe autonomy by default) is served by rendering every unknown as unknown rather than as a plausible default — the same fail-closed duty the service already meets, applied to the panel. Principle IV (human-visible auditability) is the principle the Dispatches tab exists to serve: every state readable, every reason visible, every correlation id copyable. Principle VII (thin orchestration boundary) is satisfied rather than strained — a shell adds no host capability, and the removal of the manual *Start session* control makes the boundary stricter than before. No principle is weakened.
- **Requirements explicitly unchanged**: everything in this predecessor that 005 does not name below, including 002's credential custody, trigger set, scope matrix, agent pin, bounds and delimiters, manual cleanup, and read-only-to-GitHub posture; 003's run key, coalescing, lease, dispatch token, impossibility requirement, state model, correlation model, dead-letter budget, resolution semantics, and audit vocabulary; 004's message composition, prompt bounds, credential-shape refusal, fingerprint, and snapshot rule. In particular **003 FR-028 and 003 FR-035 are strengthened**, not relaxed: 005 retires the manual dispatch control that violated them.
- **Migration impact**: none, and this is the cheapest amendment 005 could make. Nothing is implemented; no store, file, or wire payload exists to convert. Where a surface changes, a stored value continues to read as what it was (FR-005) and a pre-003 queue row continues to project through 003's migration table (FR-041).
- **Approval status**: **approved 2026-09-28** by the product owner. The four questions raised at the phase gate were confirmed against the defaults this specification had already encoded in `## Clarifications` rows 21–24; the confirmations are recorded at rows 25–28 and in `## Resolved Gate Questions`, each with the rejected alternative. **Approval changed no requirement text**, so the version stays 1.0.0 and the normative body is byte-identical to the version submitted at the gate. This is the version Phase 4 plans.

### v1.4.0 — 2026-09-30 (record amendment; the FR-057 mention-token override store does not exist in this build)

- **Rationale**: FR-057 requires the mention-token override to be **rendered per binding**, to **default to `@<login>` of the bound account**, and to be **visibly marked as an override when it differs from that default (002 FR-015)**. Implementing it in T-022 surfaced a fact the text does not carry: **the shipped service has no store for that override.** `BindingRecord` has no mention-token member; `service/poll/triggers.ts` matches with `mentionsLogin(body, binding.accountLogin)`; `grep` finds no `mentionToken` anywhere in `service/` or `src/`; an extra member on `PUT /v1/bindings` is **silently dropped** by `assembleBinding`; and 005's own approved data-model says "**005 adds no field and no endpoint (FR-050)**" (§7). 002 FR-015 grants the override with a **MAY** — "*MAY* be overridden per repository" — a permission the shipped store has never exercised. Leaving that silent would make this specification promise a control the product cannot honour, which is the defect the amendment discipline exists to prevent.
- **Sequencing decision**: this is a **record amendment**, exactly as v1.1.0 was for 006's editability and v1.3.0 was for the row count. **FR-057's text is retained verbatim and its observable guarantee is unchanged**: the binding editor renders `@${binding.accountLogin}` — precisely the value the service matches on — defaults it to `@<login>` of the bound account, and marks it as an override when the two differ (upstream rename drift, or any future stored override), so an operator who has never seen the field can still tell that a binding matches on something other than its own login. What is **deferred** is the *stored* per-binding operator override: a successor feature owns the mention-token store, and the **lifting condition is that the service must both store *and* consume it** — a member `mentionsLogin` never reads is an inert setting, which is the failure mode 006 exists to eliminate. The entry therefore records an **unimplemented permission inherited from 002 FR-015's *MAY***, **not a weakening of FR-057**.
- **Principles reviewed, unchanged in substance**: Principle II (safe autonomy by default) is the reason an unconsumed field is refused rather than shipped: a control the panel displays and the service ignores would misstate what the operator can decide. Principle IV (human-visible auditability) is served *better* by the derived display, because the operator reads the exact value the dispatch trail matched on rather than a stored intention nobody applied. Principle VI (specification and verification before implementation) is what this entry is: the gap was found during implementation and recorded, not narrowed in silence. Principle VII (thin orchestration boundary) is unstrained — no capability, host API, storage key, or wire member is added. No principle is weakened, and **no requirement text changes**.

- **Why a disabled binding gets a counterfactual rather than silence** (clarification row 45). Saying only *"this binding is off"* would satisfy NFR-114 and tell the operator nothing about the thing they are one click from changing: two rows reading identically can be one re-enable away from opposite exposures, and the operator is contemplating exactly that click. FR-092 is about **loudness, tone, and location** — a warning that is visible, worded, not an error, and present on every surface that lists a binding. ### v1.15.0 — 2026-10-03 (the list's comma is length-dependent; FR-096's composition rule, GitHub issue #9)

- **Rationale**: FR-096's **normative prose** and its own **summary row** disagreed about the list's punctuation. The prose said *joined by `or` with a comma before every phrase but the last*, which yields `a, or b` for two phrases — a comma in front of *or* with only one item before it, which is not how English writes a two-item list. The same requirement's summary said only *joined by `or`*, which reads either way. The implementation followed the prose and shipped `a, or b`. **The product owner has ruled for the standard English convention: the comma is length-dependent — exactly two phrases take none before `or`; three or more take one.**

  This is the **third** time this branch has had to reconcile a copy divergence between a requirement's prose and its rendering, and the second time the resolution was a correction rather than a cut (v1.13.0 removed a falsehood about the provider, v1.14.0 removed one about the binding). The pattern is the reason this is an amendment rather than a copy ticket: **the prose and the code must move together, or the next reader has to re-derive which of the two the panel was built from.** Recording it here is what stops the divergence from surviving a third round.

- **Product-owner decision, ratified**: *the comma is length-dependent.* Nothing below re-litigates it; it is recorded as encoded.

- **Why the rule is length-dependent rather than a blanket change.** English punctuation puts the comma **before the last item of a list of three or more**, because the last item closes a series the reader has been tracking: `a, b, or c`. With only **two** items there is no series to close, and the comma before *or* is not idiomatic — `a or b`, not `a, or b`. A blanket *drop the comma* ruling was considered and **rejected**: it would have fixed the two-phrase case by breaking the three-phrase case, and `a b or c` — neither idiomatic nor a list — is worse to read than either. So the ruling follows the convention, and the join asks **how many phrases it holds**, not only which one is last.

> **v1.5.0 (2026-09-30) — post-install review: the consent step's acceptance was not observed.** The product owner installed the build and reported the service-capability prerequisite stuck on *not met* — "the in-panel consent step … has not been accepted" — after accepting it on Accounts. **No requirement changes**, because the text already forbids this: FR-037 requires an unmet prerequisite the panel *can* determine to raise the notice and a met-and-checkable one never to nag; User Story 7 says the ones the panel cannot check say so; NFR-112 makes *not checkable* an honest value of its own; AC-110 and AC-111 pin both halves of the notice rule. What was broken was the **observation**: (a) the accept path repainted the handoff group but not the prerequisites above the tab strip, so a correct acceptance never reached the line it should have cleared; (b) the consent step was hidden once an account was connected, so an install whose account was adopted after a reinstall could not reach the acceptance at all; and (c) the service answer was read as "a pre-flight ran" rather than "an answer arrived", so an unreachable service reported the false *not met* "the service answered, but its store is not writable" instead of *not checkable*. All three are fixed, and `## Clarifications` row 32 records the finding. **Rejected alternative: new requirement text for the consent rule** — inventing a requirement for something FR-037 already says would split one obligation across two places and let them drift.

The vocabulary block's own module, `src/vocabulary.ts`, is deleted with it, which is why the L1 scan now exempts nothing. **Rejected alternative: keeping the four statements behind the disclosure too** — the owner's ruling was removal, not deferral, and a disclosure that hides prose nobody asked for is a second place for that prose to drift. `## Clarifications` row 33 records the ruling.
